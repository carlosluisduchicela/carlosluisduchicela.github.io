lyft · toledo · driver pay optimization

what should drivers be paid so the marketplace clears on both sides?

one route — toledo airport ↔ downtown. the rider fare is fixed at $25. the only lever is how much of it the driver gets.

the short version. the model and the full write-up are linked from the site.

01 · the trade

every dollar is a trade between match and margin

$19 → 60%
driver pay → match rate
$22 → 93%
driver pay → match rate

pay too little and riders can't get a car. pay too much and lyft's take per trip disappears. the optimum is in between — and it is a number, not a feeling.

02 · the model

a steady-state model of the whole route

parametervalue
rider base10,000 / month, topped back up as riders churn
rider churn10% if matched · 33% if no car comes
match ratelinear in pay: m(x) = 0.11x − 1.49
rider cac$15 per replaced rider
driver cac$500 · 5% monthly driver churn · 100 rides/driver/month

unmatched riders are the expensive ones — they churn at 3× the rate and have to be bought back.

03 · the answer
$20.87

per trip to the driver. that is where lyft's net revenue on the route peaks in the steady state — high enough to keep matches (and riders) up, low enough to keep a real take on the $25 fare.

04 · why not the edges

both edges lose money, differently

  • below $20.87 — matches fall, unmatched riders churn at 33%, and replacement cac eats what the lower pay saved.
  • above $20.87 — match gains flatten while the take per trip keeps shrinking. you are buying matches you mostly already had.
05 · the honest caveat

steady state is a model, not the weather

the number holds while the assumptions do. seasonality, a shift in driver supply, or churn moving off baseline means re-running it — the model is the deliverable as much as the $20.87 is.
